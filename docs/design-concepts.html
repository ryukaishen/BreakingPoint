BreakingPoint: two directions for the redesign

Both directions share one product rethink built on the audit and your answers: athletes first, coaches second, long-term progression third. The breaking point is a warning, never an achievement. What we celebrate is sustainable improvement, measured from your own data. They differ in how loudly the interface speaks.

All numbers in the mock screens are sample data for a synthetic athlete, labeled as such in each frame. Real records will come only from live sessions. Nothing here is implemented yet.

What the audit found

Impeccable's design review scored the current interface 25 / 40 on Nielsen's heuristics: acceptable, with the weakest marks on real-world language, error prevention, flexibility and minimalism. It credits the workflow-as-navigation track, the honest data-backed claims and the Form drawdown chart. It also says the visual language reads as a generic dark sports-tech dashboard: the Solo Leveling cue barely registers and the Blue Lock energy is absent.

25 / 40

Impeccable's mechanical detector agrees the surface is mostly clean: 2 findings in source (a 2px accent border on formula blocks, a height transition on the depth gauge). On the live session page it adds two glows on active indicators, depth-gauge and chart labels under 11 px, and a skipped heading level on sport pages. Four browser hits were false positives: deliberate status-code capitals, chart annotations and the rep label.

  1. No athlete-at-distance view. From 3 m only the rep number and skeleton colour are readable; the athlete walks back to the laptop four times per session; there are no audio cues.
  2. The breaking point over-alarms and under-guides. About 12 surfaces turn red at once, the next step is below the fold, and "End set" is styled as destructive.
  3. Data-integrity traps. Baselines and personal bests are keyed by movement, not athlete; reset has no confirmation; the logo silently abandons a live set.
  4. Detector internals are the athlete's vocabulary. EWMA, CUSUM, σ RMS and μ₀ ± σ₀ appear by default, with two unit systems.
  5. The progression loop has no shape. No rest step, contradictory recovery copy, recovery reps never charted, no session-complete moment, no history.

The product rethink both directions share

The visual direction is the smaller decision. These changes come from the audit and your answers, and they apply whichever direction you pick.

HomeYour next session, current records, this week against your own weekly goal, recent sessions. First run is onboarding: pick a sport, calibrate.
TrainSport → protocol → a session that runs hands-free: athlete view at distance, evidence for coaches one tap away.
DebriefA full screen after the set replaces the summary modal: result, next step, comparison with last time, what changed, records earned.
ProgressPer protocol: records, history charts, consistency against your goal, milestones with their exact criteria.

During the workout: quiet

  • Athlete view. Stage-dominant layout, one state word large enough for 3 m, one instruction, and reps held at your baseline as the hero number with your best marked.
  • Hands-free flow. Calibration auto-advances to the monitored set after a short countdown; optional spoken cues for baseline locked, drift, and "end the set and rest" (browser speech, no new dependency).
  • One alarm, one instruction. At the breaking point a single red signal and a calm sentence; "End set" becomes the primary action, not a danger button.
  • Rest is a stage. Recover shows rest time elapsed (no prescribed duration), then the 3-rep check, then one consistent result, plotted against your baseline.
  • Evidence drawer. EWMA, CUSUM and the drift score move behind "Show evidence" for coaches; athletes see "margin to your line" in plain words.

After the workout: earned drama

  • Records. Most reps held at baseline, best recovery return, tightest baseline (most consistent fresh reps). A record only counts when the set's capture quality and calibration pass a floor, so a sloppy baseline cannot inflate it.
  • Comparisons. This set against your previous session on the same protocol: held reps, drift onset, recovery.
  • Goals you set. A weekly session target that respects rest days. No daily streaks.
  • Milestones with criteria. Baseline locked, new record, back to baseline (the engine's own "recovered" status), goal weeks met. Each shows exactly how it was earned.
  • Never rewarded. Reaching the breaking point, reps after drift, volume, short rests, or anything that compares you with other people.

Foundations for later

  • Storage keyed by athlete × protocol, with an athlete switcher; confirmations on reset and on leaving a live set; baselines show whose they are and when they were made.
  • A versioned on-device session log (still no backend) that a later coach or squad layer can read without a rewrite.
  • Stable moves off green to the system blue, so acid lime means exactly one thing: you set a new record.

Direction A: Ascent (refined)

A precise training instrument with a single progression line running through it. Solo Leveling leads: calm system panels, clear status, the feeling of steady advancement. Blue Lock appears only as conviction in the type.

Signature move: the ascent line

Your record for reps held at baseline is drawn as a staircase. Every step up is a real new record, and the line is the same object everywhere: the Home hero, the Progress chart, and the small line in the debrief that grows one step when you beat your best. Steps never go down; a weaker session is a dot below the line, not a fall.

Type and colour

Mona Sans only, upright. Condensed widths for numerals, normal widths for text; one family keeps the instrument feel. Colour strategy is restrained: a navy rail, charcoal work areas, cobalt for actions and your data, icy cyan only for focus, lime only on the record number.

Motion

Ordinary interactions change colour in 120 ms and nothing moves. State changes cross-fade the stage band. One authored moment: when you set a record, the ascent line draws its new step and the number counts up once. Reduced motion shows the final state.

Honest risk

It may feel too calm: the Blue Lock intensity you asked for is mostly absent, and a record night looks only modestly different from an ordinary one.

Direction B: Breakaway (kinetic)

A performance broadcast about one athlete. Blue Lock leads the energy: heavy italic numerals, cobalt slabs and a single 12° cut that runs through the whole system. Solo Leveling supplies the architecture: stages, records and earned milestones.

Signature move: the record line

Borrowed from the world-record line in swimming broadcasts. In the set, a thin ghost marker shows your best reps held at baseline on a lane that fills as you hold form. In the debrief, the set replays as rep-by-rep splits against your best session; when you beat it, the result slab breaks away on its 12° cut and the new number lands. The marker always means your own record, never anyone else's.

Type and colour

Anybody in condensed black italic for numerals, state words and record headlines only; Mona Sans for every sentence, label and control so data stays legible. Colour strategy is committed: cobalt carries 30–50% of post-workout screens as slabs; black and navy hold the rest; lime appears only on a new record.

Motion

Quiet by default: hover and press change colour only. In the set, a state change sweeps the cut edge once (180 ms). After the set, one choreographed moment per earned record: the slab wipes in along the 12° cut, the numeral lands with a short settle, the lime tag follows. It never fires for the breaking point. Reduced motion shows the final state.

Honest risk

The most memorable direction, but the easiest to overdo. Italic condensed numerals lose legibility below about 28 px, so they stay out of tables. Angled cuts complicate responsive layouts and focus outlines. Without discipline it drifts toward a gaming skin.

My recommendation

Direction B, held to A's discipline in the set. Its record line is the strongest expression of "you against your past self", and it gives the post-workout moments the intensity you asked for. To protect usability, the athlete view keeps B's type and colour but A's quietness: no slabs, one state plate, the lane, nothing else. The 12° angle is the only geometric motif, and condensed italic stays out of tables and body text.
